You open the fridge and set what you need straight onto the counter beside it — still under the window, still in daylight.

02 — Wash

A few steps to the sink, not a walk. Whatever's chopped gets rinsed right there, and the stone keeps it quiet.

01

Multipurpose sink

The basin is shaped so water naturally runs off anything placed in it, rather than pooling. Stack a washed plate to one side and by the time you've moved to the next, the first is already dry — no wiping down the rack afterward.

02

Arch window, kept clear

Most kitchens box a window like this in with cabinets for extra storage. Here that trade was made the other way — the window stays completely open, remaining the visual centerpiece rather than being chopped up by cabinet doors. Because it also opens, it doubles as ventilation while you work at the sink.

03

Purifier & dedicated tap

A water purifier sits in the cabinet below the sink, connected to its own separate tap. Drinking water is always exactly where you're already standing — no bottles to refill, no filter jug taking up fridge space.

04

Stone sink, not steel

Because the sink is stone rather than steel, everyday use — rinsing a pot, running the tap — doesn't produce the sharp metallic clang steel sinks are known for. A quieter kitchen overall, which matters most if this space opens into your living or dining area.

05

Hidden bin, wet & dry

Open the cabinet door beneath the counter and the bin is right there, split into two. Wet waste and dry waste separate themselves the moment you throw something away, instead of needing to be sorted later — and there's no separate bin sitting out on the floor.

06

Wicker basket

Onions, potatoes, and garlic need airflow to stay fresh — sealed away, they sweat and spoil faster. This ventilated basket lets air move through, sits low, and pulls out fully, so a glance tells you exactly what's left instead of digging through a dark cabinet.

07

Blind corner, solved

Every U-shaped kitchen has one corner that's naturally hard to reach — deep, dark, usually wasted. Here it pulls outward on its own, bringing everything stored in it into full view instead of leaving it pushed back and forgotten.

01

Wall cabinets beside the hood

These two cabinets sit at exactly the height and distance your hand naturally reaches while standing at the hob. The idea is to keep your daily-use spices, oils, and condiments here — not pantry stock — so you're never turning away from a dish on the flame to find what you need.

02

Bottle pull-out

A slim, dedicated pull-out beside the hob holds oil, vinegar, and the masalas you reach for constantly while cooking. Slide it open, take what you need, slide it shut — all without leaning across a lit flame.

03

Ladder pull-out in the tall unit

Top shelves in a tall unit are usually a "get the stool" problem. Here, a built-in step slides outward from the unit itself, turning the top shelf into something just as easy to reach as the bottom one.

04

Cutlery & thali drawer

Rather than a shelf, this is a soft-close pull-out drawer positioned directly below the hob, divided into sections for spoons, forks, and thalis — sturdy enough to hold a full stack of steel thalis without sagging, and nothing at the back gets lost since it glides out completely.

05

Loft storage

Above the cabinets, there's a dedicated space for things you only need occasionally — festival cookware, extra dabbas, appliances that come out a few times a year — keeping your everyday shelves uncluttered.

06

Hob & chimney

The hob sits directly beneath the chimney, so smoke and steam are pulled away right where they're created rather than drifting through the rest of the house — no waving smoke off, no smell lingering an hour later.

LOOK & FEEL

White, wood, and one black line running through it all.

The same choices repeat across all three walls — which is exactly what makes it read as one kitchen, not three separate zones.

01

Glossy acrylic shutters

Every base cabinet shutter is glossy acrylic, chosen to stay low-maintenance. No grain or texture to trap grease or fingerprints — a wipe with a damp cloth is usually enough, especially useful right beside the hob.

02

Black G-profile handles

The same black handle repeats on every wall — sink cabinets, cook wall drawers, tall storage units. A small, quiet detail, but the one visual thread that ties three separate walls into a single, cohesive kitchen.

03

White with wood accents

The base palette stays white throughout, with wood tones reserved mainly for the tall storage columns — a subtle bit of visual logic that quietly reinforces how the kitchen is organized.

04

Soft-close, without exception

Every shutter and every drawer across all three walls closes on its own, gently, every time — not just on the showcase pieces. Nothing in daily use here ever slams.

LIGHTING

Light in layers, not one flat overhead beam.

Three distinct light sources, each doing a different job — mood, room, and task.

01

Profile light (ceiling perimeter)

A light strip traces the edge of the ceiling around the kitchen — ambient, mood lighting that softens the room in the evening rather than lighting any single task.

02

Surface lights (4 points)

Four surface-mounted lights handle the kitchen's actual day-to-day illumination, working alongside the profile light rather than replacing it — general daily lighting, tone still to be decided.

03

Under-cabinet task lighting

A separate strip runs beneath the wall cabinets above the cook and cutting counters, lighting the counter surface directly — so your own hands and head never cast a shadow over what you're chopping or plating.
